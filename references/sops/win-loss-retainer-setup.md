# SOP: Setting up a new win/loss retainer client

**Owner:** Iris Okonjo (Senior Analyst). Iris designed this process and is the one Tomas
currently has to ask, client by client, when it isn't written down anywhere. This file exists
so he stops having to ask.

**When to run it:** the moment a win/loss retainer contract is signed, before the first
interview is scheduled. Not for a one-off buyer-interview or landscape-study engagement —
those have their own scoping in the project brief, not this SOP.

## Steps

1. **Pull the closed-deal list from the client, not from memory.** Ask the client's deal desk
   or RevOps contact for every closed-won and closed-lost opportunity from the last 90 days,
   with deal size and close date. Do not start from whichever deals the client's champion
   happens to remember — that list is always skewed toward recent, dramatic losses and
   under-represents quiet, boring wins.
   *If the client can't produce a clean list:* that's a scoping flag, not a blocker. Ana
   reviews it before interviews are scheduled — a client without deal-level visibility usually
   needs a shorter first month to prove the format before locking a 90-day list.

2. **Set the interview quota before naming names.** Default is 6 interviews a month — split
   evenly between won and lost unless the client's win rate makes that impossible, in which
   case skew toward whichever side is thinner in the deal list. Confirm the split with the
   client's sponsor before Tomas starts outreach, not after.

3. **Tomas sends the scheduling outreach within 2 business days of the quota being confirmed.**
   Use the standard win/loss ask — never send the "why did you choose us / not choose us"
   framing as a cold open, it reads as a sales save-attempt and tanks response rate. Lead with
   "we're doing an independent review of how the deal went," not with the client's brand name.
   *If a respondent doesn't reply within 5 business days:* one follow-up, then move on. A
   retainer runs monthly — a slow respondent this month is a candidate next month, not a
   reason to hold the whole batch.

4. **Every interview gets recorded and transcribed before synthesis starts. No exceptions.**
   This is not specific to win/loss — it's the standing rule in
   `knowledge/playbook/record-before-synthesize.md` — but it matters most here, because a
   win/loss finding gets read by the client's own sales leadership and a misremembered quote
   about why a deal was lost is the fastest way to lose the retainer itself.
   *If a respondent won't consent to recording:* the interview still happens, but Iris sits in
   and takes verbatim notes live — Tomas does not run an unrecorded win/loss interview solo.

5. **Code each transcript against the standing taxonomy, not a fresh one per client.** The
   taxonomy (price, product gap, timing, champion turnover, competitor-specific loss) lives
   with Iris and gets reused across every retainer client so patterns are comparable
   month over month. A client-specific taxonomy is what makes month 6 impossible to compare
   against month 1.

6. **Monthly synthesis note goes to the client 5 business days after the last interview in
   that batch closes**, not on a fixed calendar date — the batch, not the calendar, sets the
   deadline. Iris reviews every synthesis note before it goes out for the first three months
   of a new retainer; after that, Tomas can send directly and loop Iris in only when a finding
   contradicts a pattern from a prior month.
   *If the batch runs short (fewer than 4 completed interviews):* say so in the note instead
   of padding the finding with a thin sample. A retainer client trusts the read more when a
   thin month is labeled thin.

## What this doesn't cover

Fee structure, contract terms, and renewal timing live in the client's project file and in
`knowledge/playbook/hold-the-fee-negotiate-scope.md` — this SOP is the operating cadence once
the retainer is signed, not how it gets sold.
